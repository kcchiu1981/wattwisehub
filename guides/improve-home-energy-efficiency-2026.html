How to Improve Home Energy Efficiency in 2026: Practical Tips for US Homeowners and Renters

In 2026, improving your home's energy efficiency not only helps reduce utility bills but also increases comfort and supports environmental sustainability. Whether you own your home or rent, there are effective and practical steps you can take to create a more energy-wise household.

Why Energy Efficiency Matters

Energy-efficient homes use less electricity, natural gas, or other fuels to maintain comfortable temperatures, power appliances, and provide lighting. This can result in lower energy expenses and a smaller carbon footprint. Small improvements can add up over time, and many are budget-friendly or easy to implement.

Practical Steps to Boost Your Home’s Energy Efficiency

  1. Conduct an Energy Audit: Start by assessing how energy is used and identifying leaks or inefficiencies. Many utility companies in the US offer free or low-cost home energy audits, or you can use a DIY approach with a thermal camera or blower door test.
  2. Seal Drafts and Install Weatherstripping: Air leaks around windows, doors, and other openings are common sources of heat loss or gain. Adding weatherstripping or caulking can improve insulation and comfort. For detailed guidance, see our Weatherstripping Guide.
  3. Upgrade to a Smart Thermostat: Smart thermostats help optimize heating and cooling by learning your schedule and preferences. They can reduce wasted energy by adjusting temperatures when you're away. Learn more in our Smart Thermostat Guide.
  4. Improve Insulation: Insulating attics, walls, and floors minimizes heat transfer. Even adding insulation to accessible areas like attic hatches or crawlspaces can help. If you rent, check with your landlord about possible improvements.
  5. Use Energy-Efficient Lighting and Appliances: Replace incandescent bulbs with ENERGY STAR® certified LEDs and choose energy-efficient appliances when replacing old ones to reduce electricity use.
  6. Adjust Your Water Heating: Lower your water heater’s temperature to 120°F and insulate the water heater tank. Consider installing low-flow showerheads and faucet aerators to reduce hot water use.
  7. Leverage Natural Ventilation and Shade: Use windows strategically to allow cooling breezes in warmer months and block direct sunlight with curtains or shades. Planting deciduous trees can also provide shade in summer and sun exposure in winter.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can renters make energy-efficient upgrades?
Yes, renters can often make simple changes like adding weatherstripping, using smart thermostats (if permitted), and switching to energy-efficient lighting. Always check with your landlord before making more significant alterations.
Is it worth investing in a smart thermostat?
For many homeowners, smart thermostats provide an easy way to reduce heating and cooling costs while improving comfort. They are especially beneficial if you have varying schedules or want to automate temperature control.
How do I know if my home is well insulated?
Signs of poor insulation include uneven temperatures indoors, frequent drafts, and high heating or cooling costs. An energy audit or professional inspection can give a clear assessment.
Are energy-efficient upgrades expensive?
Costs vary widely. Simple fixes like weatherstripping are low-cost, while insulation or HVAC upgrades require larger investments. Many upgrades qualify for rebates or tax incentives at federal or state levels, which can help offset expenses.
