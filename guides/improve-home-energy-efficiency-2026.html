How to Improve Home Energy Efficiency in 2026

Improving home energy efficiency is not about buying every new device. The best results usually come from finding the biggest sources of waste first, then fixing drafts, runtime, lighting, and high-use appliances in the right order.

This guide is written for US homeowners and renters who want practical, lower-risk ways to reduce electricity waste and improve comfort in 2026.

Start With Measurement Before Buying Anything

Before replacing devices or adding smart-home gear, identify where energy is actually being used. A simple DIY audit can help you find the top one to three problems instead of guessing.

  • Check your utility bill for seasonal spikes.
  • Look for always-on devices, old appliances, and long AC runtime.
  • Use a plug-in electricity monitor for individual devices.
  • Walk around windows, exterior doors, and attic access points to feel for drafts.

If you are not sure where to begin, start with our DIY Home Energy Audit.

1. Seal Drafts Before Optimizing the Thermostat

Draft sealing is often one of the best first steps because it improves comfort and helps your heating or cooling system work less. Focus first on exterior doors, window frames, attic hatches, and visible gaps.

For a step-by-step approach, read the Weatherstripping Guide.

2. Use Smart Thermostat Settings Carefully

A smart thermostat can help, but only if the schedule matches your real routine. Avoid extreme temperature swings and focus on reducing runtime when the home is empty or overnight comfort needs are lower.

If you already have one, review your schedule using our Smart Thermostat Guide.

3. Replace the Most-Used Bulbs First

Do not worry about replacing every bulb at once. Start with lights used for several hours per day, such as kitchen, living room, hallway, porch, or home office bulbs.

4. Reduce Plug Load and Standby Waste

Many homes waste electricity through devices that stay on all day. Entertainment centers, desktop setups, chargers, networking gear, and older appliances are worth checking.

Use the Electricity Cost Calculator to estimate whether a device is worth replacing, unplugging, or putting on a smart plug.

5. Adjust Water Heating

Water heating can be a meaningful part of household energy use. Consider a safe temperature setting, insulating accessible hot-water pipes, using low-flow fixtures, and fixing leaks quickly.

For Renters: Focus on Reversible Fixes

Renters should prioritize upgrades that are removable and low-risk:

  • Foam weatherstripping tape
  • Door draft stoppers
  • LED bulbs
  • Smart plugs with energy monitoring
  • Thermal curtains
  • Basic outlet timers

Always check your lease or ask your landlord before changing thermostats, wiring, insulation, or permanent fixtures.

FAQ

What is the best first step to improve home energy efficiency?

Start with a basic energy audit. It helps you find the biggest waste first, instead of spending money on upgrades that may not matter much for your home.

Are smart thermostats worth it?

They can be worth it if you have central HVAC, a consistent schedule, and use the settings correctly. They are less useful if your main issue is poor insulation or air leaks.

What can renters do without major changes?

Renters can usually start with weatherstripping, LED bulbs, smart plugs, curtains, and better appliance habits. Avoid permanent changes without permission.

Should I buy products immediately?

Not always. Measure first, then buy only what solves a confirmed problem.
